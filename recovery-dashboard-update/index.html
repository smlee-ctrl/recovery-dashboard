Connections

Bring in WHOOP, Apple Health and your calendar. Each source can import an export file, or connect live where a connector exists. Switch between the two with the toggle on each card.

WHOOP

Built-in export

In the WHOOP app, find Data Export in your account settings and request your data. WHOOP emails you a ZIP. Upload the ZIP, or the CSV files inside it. Newer days are added, and days you already have are updated.

Coming soon

Live WHOOP sync isn’t available yet. WHOOP only opens its live data to apps it has approved, and there’s no WHOOP connector for Claude. Until there is, use Import to bring your data in.

Apple Health

Not connected

On your iPhone, open Health, tap your profile picture, then Export All Health Data. Save export.zip to Files or AirDrop it to this computer, then upload it here. Steps, active energy, sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, nutrition, water, caffeine and workouts from the last 400 days come in. Data WHOOP wrote to Apple Health is skipped so nothing counts twice.

Coming soon

Apple Health only shares live data with apps on your iPhone, so a web dashboard can’t connect to it directly. A companion iPhone app could sync it here later. Until then, use Import with your Health export.

Google Calendar

Not connected

In Google Calendar on a computer, open Settings, then Import & export, and choose Export. Unzip the download and upload the .ics file for your main calendar. Apple Calendar and Outlook exports work too. Only start and end times are kept, never titles or guests.

Reads your Google Calendar live through your Claude connector, from six weeks back to two weeks ahead. Busy days feed into Stress and What’s holding you back, free time is used to suggest training times, and you can add sessions to your calendar. Event titles stay on your calendar and aren’t saved here.

Imported data is saved to your account and private to you. Your export files are read in your browser and aren’t uploaded. A connected calendar is read each time you open the page and isn’t stored.

How this dashboard works
Your WHOOP data
Built from your export of June 11 to November 5, 2026. Readiness is WHOOP's Recovery score and Sleep is WHOOP's Sleep performance, shown as is. Days after November 5 have logs only until a new export is added.
Your logs
Each tab works like a diary for the day picked in the week strip: summary cards up top, then rows you tap Log on to add an entry, or the dots to see and edit what you logged. Workouts can be tagged with a coach (Tanner Newman, Charlie Keane or Dillon Johnson). Saved to your account and private to you. Food, workouts and sleep notes feed the analysis and the body map; they never change WHOOP's own scores.
What's holding you back
Shown on mornings under 67. Checks late bedtime, short sleep, low HRV, raised resting heart rate, hard training, alcohol, late caffeine, late meals, high sodium, low fluids, sleep debt, temperature or breathing changes, and the sleep notes you logged. Food and workouts count from the day before; sleep notes from the night you woke up from. These are likely causes, not a diagnosis.
Arranging tiles
Press and hold any tile for a moment, then drag it. Every tile then shows a width handle on its right edge and a Hide button; drag any of them straight away. Tap Done, press Esc, or tap outside the tiles to finish. Keyboard users can Tab to "Arrange tiles" at the top.
Connections
Each card has an Import / Connect toggle. WHOOP and Apple Health import export files; live connections for them are coming soon, since neither offers one to a web page. Google Calendar connects live through your Claude connector, or imports an .ics file. WHOOP data replaces or extends the built-in export by date. Apple Health fills in steps, active energy, sleep, HRV and resting heart rate, nutrition and workouts. Apple measures HRV as SDNN and WHOOP as RMSSD, so each is only compared with itself. Apple workouts have no effort score, so they count as moderate effort.
Calendar and stress
Your calendar is turned into a busyness score for each day: hours scheduled past four, events ending after 9 pm, and starts before 7:30 am. A packed day adds to the next morning’s stress estimate (the pink “Busy calendar” segment) and can show up in What’s holding you back. All-day events, declined invites and events marked free don’t count.
Training times
Free gaps between 6 am and 10 pm, with 20 minutes of buffer around events and at least two hours before your usual bedtime. Session length follows your latest Readiness: 90 minutes when recovered, 60 when recovering, 40 for light work when under 34. Late afternoon is ranked first for strength work.
Tile sizes
While arranging, drag a tile’s right edge to resize it, or hide tiles you don’t need. A score widget one column wide shows the score; two or three columns add the headline or key numbers; full width adds contributors and the last 7 days. Your layout is saved to your account. With a keyboard, focus a handle and use the arrow keys.
Muscle readiness
An estimate from training load, not a measurement. Each exercise loads muscles by a fixed share; each muscle recovers at its own rate.
Training load
Session load is duration × effort (session RPE). Over 600 counts as a hard day.
Estimated 1RM
Load × (1 + (reps + reps in reserve) ÷ 30), where reps in reserve is 10 minus RPE.
Rep slowdown
From reps you mark on the video: 1 minus the first rep's lifting time divided by the last rep's.
Videos
Stored with this dashboard, up to 20 MB each. Anyone you share the dashboard with in your organization could open a video's link.
